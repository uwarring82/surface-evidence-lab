10 September 2026 · First-pass scout · Prepared by Codex

**Recommendation:** build a two-case teaching core: Seibert et al. (2020) for a controlled, same-area intervention with variable outcomes, and Croshaw et al. (2020) for measurement-induced specimen changes. Croshaw has a CC BY reuse basis. (Correction, 17 September 2026: the recorded CC BY basis for Seibert was unsupported and is withdrawn; Seibert's redistribution permission is unverified, so it is a linked reading until permission for the exact version and figure is verified.) Use Xu (2024) and Lu (2014) as linked readings with separate commentary for the timed sequence and historical interpretation. Their images will not be reproduced or adapted in the public core.

This recommendation changes the first exercise's physical question to **gas-derived structures versus organic contamination**. It does not deliver a bulk-diffusion-versus-ambient-contamination case. If that exact distinction is mandatory for the first lab, the shortlist remains provisional.

This is a focused scout, not the completed five-person-day survey and not the completed teaching packet. The [revised task card](../planning/task-card.md) explicitly accepts scout-level coverage as a completed selection milestone; any formal bounded survey remains a separate, open milestone. A [worksheet](../teaching/student-worksheet.md) and [instructor's analysis](../teaching/instructor-notes.md) are now available as review drafts. Several candidates are useful; none has been shown to meet every criterion of the narrow research question.

## What was checked

Searches began with H-terminated Si(111), ambient oxidation, AFM, same-area observations, diffusion, and contamination. Citation following extended into graphite–water stripes, silicon defect classification, and specimen histories through surface treatments. Sources included publisher articles, institutional repositories, arXiv, Europe PMC and a Figshare deposit record. Primary sources support the findings below; aggregators served only as discovery leads.

Full text was inspected for Seibert 2020, Xu 2024, Lu 2014, Pálinkás 2022, Croshaw 2020, Wanjura 2019, Angermann 2011 and a 2026 graphite study. Seibert Fig. 2, Croshaw Fig. 5 and Pálinkás Fig. 1 were also visually inspected in downloaded PDFs. Other leads are explicitly marked as abstract-level or incomplete. Sources were retrieved on 10 September 2026; successful downloads have UTC timestamps and SHA-256 hashes in the source manifest.

## 1. Recommended core: the graphite stripe dispute

**Seibert, Klassen, Latus, Bechstein and Kühnle, “Origin of Ubiquitous Stripes at the Graphite–Water Interface,” Langmuir 36, 7789–7794 (2020).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.langmuir.0c00748) · [Bielefeld/NOAH repository](https://noah.nrw/ubbihs/content/titleinfo/5141652)

The decisive exercise uses Fig. 2 and its discussion on pp. 7790–7791. A stripe-free interface prepared with a glass syringe is imaged, water is exchanged through a plastic syringe, and stripes appear. The text explicitly identifies the same area; terrace geometry is visible in both panels. This is a supported spatial claim and a documented intervention, not just two representative samples. The paper reports seven glass-syringe trials without stripes and two repetitions of the glass-to-plastic exchange with matching outcomes.

Stripes are absent in approximately one fifth of the broader plastic-syringe experiments. This variability is essential to the worksheet: it contrasts with the seven negative glass references and prevents a successful image pair from becoming a universal rule. The two exchange trials are a distinct protocol, not evidence that their failure probability is exactly 20%. The chemical identity remains uncertain, but this identification question does not substitute for an unresolved relationship claim. In-situ exchange provides little challenge for characterising long history gaps; that safeguard remains separately unmet. This scout originally stated that the repository explicitly assigns CC BY 4.0. Correction, 17 September 2026: that statement is withdrawn. The preserved "rights record" is a browser-verification page, and the PDF carries an ACS copyright notice with no Creative Commons notice. Redistribution permission is unverified; see the [manifest](source-manifest.md).

**Xu, Qiao, Nan and Bao, “Environmental Influence on Stripe Formation at the Graphite–Water Interface,” ChemPhysChem 25, e202400641 (2024).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cphc.202400641) · [Full text](https://chemistry-europe.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cphc.202400641)

Fig. 4 and §2.3 explicitly report revisiting the same scan area at 30 and 170 min. Fig. 2 also includes water-contact evolution and a scan-angle check. Nitrogen, argon, wet-lab and cleanroom conditions provide comparisons supporting the authors' contamination interpretation. The relative roles of direct adsorption and gas-assisted transport remain unresolved.

Two Fig. 4 caption errors are diagnosable from the XML: the adhesive partner of panel (e) is (f), not the repeated (b); that of (g) is (h), not the repeated (d). The body text uses 4f and 4g–h. These editorial corrections are recorded separately from unchanged source bytes, not as a published erratum. They do not verify that 4e revisits 4a to a stated spatial tolerance across the reported interval (140 minutes only if both times count from immersion, which the caption does not state; corrected 17 September 2026). That correspondence is the unresolved relationship for the linked exercise; drift is not quantified by the supplied evidence.

Full text/captions are preserved as Europe PMC XML; its licence states **CC BY-NC 4.0**. The PDF endpoint returned a non-PDF response. Raw data are offered on request, not deposited openly here. Xu remains link-only in the public core. No author contact was made.

**Lu et al., “Interface-Induced Ordering of Gas Molecules Confined in a Small Space,” Scientific Reports 4, 7189 (2014).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep07189)

Use Fig. 1 for reported temporal/spatial correspondence and Figs. 2–3 for probe-force interventions. The proposed composition is condensed gas. This supplies a real earlier interpretation to compare with the later contamination evidence. The article states **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**; retain an unmodified source or link and place original teaching commentary separately. Do not assume permission for adapted figure composites.

**Historical safeguard:** these are real, differently dated scientific claims. They can support a history of interpretations about a phenomenon. They are not evidence that a later group reanalysed the same raw image or the same specimen. A revision of an exact record-to-record correspondence remains unlocated. Neither the publication date nor the scout's retrieval date is the measurement date.

## 2. Second core case and useful extensions

**Pálinkás et al., “The composition and structure of the ubiquitous hydrocarbon contamination on van der Waals materials,” Nature Communications 13, 6770 (2022).** [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34641-7) · [Version 1 data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.21294831.v1)

This adds chemical and structural evidence to a morphology-based argument. Fig. 1 compares AFM/STM observations; Figs. 2–3 provide spectroscopy and molecular comparisons. Do not join these panels with a same-region edge merely because they appear together. The IR procedure also involves exfoliating a specimen to obtain a reference: same holder position does not mean unchanged surface.

The article and deposit state CC BY 4.0. The deposit API confirms version 1, a 54,034,031-byte ZIP and its supplied/computed MD5. The download timed out in this scout: the record is preserved, but the archive's contents were not inspected. It is an actionable data lead, not a validated analysis-ready dataset.

**Croshaw, Dienel, Huff and Wolkow, “Atomic defect classification of the H–Si(100) surface through multi-mode scanning probe microscopy,” Beilstein Journal of Nanotechnology 11, 1346–1360 (2020).** [Article](https://doi.org/10.3762/bjnano.11.119)

Fig. 5 provides three separate tracked sequences in which the probe changes a defect. Before/after panels explicitly show the same frames; STM versus AFM interventions and a changing tip state are part of the interpretation. This is excellent for teaching why imaging is itself an event in specimen history, although it is not a long-term exposure series.

Fig. 2h/3g–h describes a siloxane assignment replacing earlier dihydride descriptions. The paper names the earlier sources, including [O'Brien et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.64.161401) and [Ballard et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1116/1.4864302). The earlier image-level assignments have not yet been independently checked in this scout. The article and supplementary PDF are preserved under the stated CC BY 4.0 licence.

**Additional current lead:** “Probing the molecular structure at graphite–water interfaces by correlating 3D-AFM and SHINERS,” Nature Communications, published 31 January 2026. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68667-y)

Fig. 2 combines time-dependent AFM and vibrational evidence; source data and unprocessed spectroscopic data are linked. Crucially, the work reports independent measurements in Madrid and Urbana: complementary methods must not be converted into a same-specimen claim. Full dataset and reuse inspection are deferred. This could strengthen the chemical analysis, but would make the introductory packet more complex.

## 3. Silicon near misses worth retaining

**Wanjura et al., “Deal-Grove Oxidation and Nanoparticle Adhesion – an AFM Study,” arXiv:1902.04494v1 (2019).** [Versioned source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04494v1)

The experimental section records wet-nitrogen desiccator storage, approximately 20 °C, and about one hour in ambient air during measurement sessions. Figs. 1 and 3–5 support local before/after particle removal and an ensemble evolution over 32 days. The authors compare linear and square-root growth models. Their procedure removes particles, so a tracked wafer history must not become a same-particle aging series. A kinetic fit alone is not independent chemical identification or discrimination of bulk-origin material from contamination. No journal version or broad reuse licence was established; keep this as a source-linked candidate.

**Angermann, Gref and Stegemann, “Effect of wet-chemical substrate smoothing on passivation of ultrathin-SiO2/n-Si(111) interfaces prepared with atomic oxygen at thermal impact energies,” Central European Journal of Physics 9, 1472–1481 (2011).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2478/s11534-011-0053-0) · [DNB full text](https://d-nb.info/1379950007/34)

§3.1.1 and Fig. 2 report a same-area comparison through oxidation, months in air, and oxide removal. This directly counters the assumption that useful histories will necessarily be absent. However, Fig. 2 is explicitly reprinted from reference 33. That source is [Angermann et al., Thin Solid Films 516, 6775–6781 (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tsf.2007.12.033). Its full text, original caption and figure rights need checking. Do not count the 2008 and 2011 publications as independent experiments. The DNB PDF was text-inspected through the web tool; local download failed certificate validation and was not retained.

**MacLaren et al., “An AFM study of the processing of hydrogen passivated silicon(111) of a low miscut angle,” Surface Science 490, 285–295 (2001).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0039-6028(01)01331-0) · [Glasgow record](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/4029/)

Relevant abstract, insufficient inspected evidence. Glasgow explicitly reports no full text. This is an access-limited lead, not evidence that the paper lacks tracking metadata.

The previously supplied **Okada et al., SDM98-173 (1998)** lead remains background only; its full Japanese report was not inspected in this scout. [IEICE record](https://www.ieice.org/publications/ken/summary.php?contribution_id=KJ00002218071&expandable=2&ken_id=SDM&lang=jp&presen_date=1998%2F12%2F10&schedule_id=AN10013254_98%28445%29&society_cd=ES&year=1998)

## Review recommendation and unmet requirements

Proceed with Seibert Fig. 2 and Croshaw Fig. 5 as the core. Xu's caption-label reconciliation is complete; assessment of the actual images remains pending. Keep Xu and Lu as linked readings with separate commentary. The older gas interpretation must retain its experimental scope; students should not be asked to declare all earlier papers wrong. See the review-draft [worksheet](../teaching/student-worksheet.md) and [instructor notes](../teaching/instructor-notes.md).

Suggested exercise sequence: identify the physical objects being compared; reconstruct the intervention; compare what the two mechanisms predict; separate observed stripes from assigned chemical identity; determine which later result can legitimately revise which earlier claim; specify the additional chemical or handling evidence needed.

The following remain **unmet**, rather than silently passed:

- An exact bulk-diffusion-versus-ambient-contamination teaching case.
- Completion of the timed same-region exercise; locating a reported sequence does not validate its correspondence.
- A real exercise of history-gap characterisation. The in-situ core cases do not stress long intervals of uncertain custody, storage or exposure.
- A demonstrated later revision to the identity/co-location link between the exact same observation records.
- Complete instrument/custody/exposure history and absolute acquisition dates.
- Quantitative registration validation or a registration certificate.
- Inspection and validation of the Figshare ZIP contents.
- A teaching walkthrough and final figure layout. Source-linked worksheet and instructor drafts are available, but not classroom-tested.

The available evidence supports a comparative teaching case and genuine tracked sequences. It does not yet satisfy all infrastructure safeguards, and no five-day null outcome is being declared.
