Surface Evidence Lab

What can a pair of surface-microscopy images establish? Working from published scanning probe experiments, students separate the evidence for a physical explanation from the evidence that two observations concern the same specimen, the same region and a documented sequence of events.

Four claims that are easy to blur

An image pair can support a mechanism and still leave open whether it shows the same specimen, the same place or an undisturbed history. Students record each as a separate claim, with its source and what remains unresolved.

MechanismPhysical explanation
Which process accounts for what is seen: adsorbed gas or contamination; hydrogen removal, a changed tip, or both?
RelationshipSpecimen identity
Do two observations concern the same specimen?
RelationshipSpatial correspondence
Do they show the same region, and to what stated tolerance?
RelationshipState history
What happened to the specimen in between, including changes caused by the measurement itself?

The same location can be in a changed state. A later interpretation of a comparable phenomenon does not overwrite an earlier claim about a different specimen.

Two core cases

Both cases are read from the published papers. Neither is a bulk-diffusion versus ambient-contamination experiment; that research question lies outside this packet.

Part A · AFM · graphite–water interface

A controlled intervention with variable outcomes

Are the stripes seen at the graphite–water interface adsorbed gas, or contamination brought in with the water?

Part B · STM and nc-AFM · H–Si(100) · 4.5 K

Measurement changes the specimen

The authors report that scanning removed a hydrogen atom from a negatively charged defect, and in one sequence changed the tip as well.
