# FP-2: Surface evidence, contamination and measurement history

Draft for review · 10 September 2026, revised 17–18 September 2026

**Physical scope:** the graphite exercise compares adsorbed-gas and organic-contamination explanations for interfacial stripes. The silicon exercise examines a probe-induced change of a defect. Neither is a bulk-diffusion-versus-ambient-contamination experiment.

**Learning aim:** distinguish evidence for a physical explanation from evidence that two observations concern the same specimen, region and documented sequence of events.

## A. A controlled intervention with variable outcomes

Read Seibert et al., *Origin of Ubiquitous Stripes at the Graphite–Water Interface*, Fig. 2 and pp. 7790–7791. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.langmuir.0c00748) (publisher version; access may require an institutional subscription)

The figure is not reproduced in this packet. Questions 2 and 4 need the paper itself: Fig. 2 for the images, and the Experimental Section for the protocol differences. If you cannot open it, say so before the session; your instructor has a description of Fig. 2 that covers part of Q2.

The reported evidence (pp. 7790–7791) is:

| Observation or protocol | Reported result |
|---|---|
| "Standard" experiments: water introduced with plastic syringes | More than 100 independent experiments; approximately 80 % showed stripes, so stripes were absent in about one fifth. |
| Water introduced using a clean glass syringe | No stripes. Because of the one-in-five absence rate, the authors repeated this experiment "seven times". Nitrogen was present. |
| Glass syringe first "cleaned" with a surfactant solution | Stripes formed again. The number of these experiments is not stated. |
| Glass-to-plastic water exchange in situ | Performed twice with matching stripe-formation outcomes. |
| Fig. 2a and 2b | Authors explicitly identify the same surface area before and after exchange; step edges are visible. |

Protocols varied across the experiments (Experimental Section, p. 7790): two AFM instruments, ultrapure water from two purification setups, some sodium chloride solutions, and in a few cases graphite kept in air before it met the solution.

The approximate fraction describes the standard plastic-syringe experiments generally. It is not an independently measured failure rate for the specific two-run exchange protocol.

1. State two competing explanations and describe what each predicts for these protocols. Distinguish a claim that nitrogen is sufficient from a claim that it contributes under additional conditions.
2. *(needs the paper)* What does the within-area Fig. 2 comparison add beyond comparing images from separate samples? Identify its correspondence evidence and its limits.
3. How does the approximate one-in-five absence rate change your reading of the successful image pair? Explain why neither two successful exchanges nor seven negative references establishes a universal rule.
4. *(needs the paper)* Which outcomes can be compared directly, and which would require information about sampling, protocol differences, independence or exact counts? The paper gives approximate totals and fractions; do not manufacture an exact contingency table from them.
5. Write a conclusion in no more than three sentences: one supported inference, one unresolved alternative, and one limit on generalising to earlier studies.
6. Propose one follow-up control that would distinguish a remaining explanation. State both possible outcomes and how each would change your inference.

Optional probability exercise: *assume*, solely for illustration, that exchanges independently produce stripes with probability 0.8. Calculate the probability of two stripe-producing exchanges. Explain why the calculation is not an estimate of the probability that the contamination explanation is true, and why the assumed probability may not transfer to this protocol.

## B. Measurement changes the specimen

Read Croshaw et al., *Atomic defect classification of the H–Si(100) surface through multi-mode scanning probe microscopy*, Fig. 5 and the accompanying discussion on p. 1356. 7. Choose one of the three sequences, (a–c), (d–f), or (g–i). Draw its event order: initial observation, probe interaction during scanning, subsequent observation. Distinguish the observed contrast change from the authors' physical assignment.
8. Record specimen identity, spatial correspondence and state history as separate claims. For each, identify the supporting source and any unresolved detail. Explain how the same physical location can have a changed physical state.

## C. Optional linked reading: a reported time series

Read Xu et al., *Environmental Influence on Stripe Formation at the Graphite–Water Interface*, Fig. 4 and §2.3. [Linked article](https://doi.org/10.1002/cphc.202400641)

Editorial note, separate from the preserved source: the Fig. 4 caption repeats earlier panel labels. The adhesive image accompanying (e) should be (f), and the adhesive image accompanying (g) should be (h), as supported by the body text. These are editorial corrections, not a published erratum. They do not validate correspondence between images.

11. The Fig. 4 caption describes 4a as the surface "immersed in water for 30 min" and 4e as the same scan area "after 170 min", without saying when the 170 minutes start. The interval is 140 minutes only if both times count from immersion; treat that as an inference, not a reported value. What supports the authors' same-area claim for 4a and 4e? What further evidence is needed to establish correspondence to a stated spatial tolerance? Treat drift as a possibility requiring assessment, not a measured fact.
12. Separate what the source reports about liquid exposure from what would be needed to rule out unrecorded changes in tip condition, positioning or handling. Distinguish unknown facts from facts contradicted by evidence.

## D. Optional linked reading: changing interpretations

Compare Lu et al., *Interface-Induced Ordering of Gas Molecules Confined in a Small Space*, *Sci. Rep.* 4, 7189 (2014), with Seibert (2020). [Lu article](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep07189)

Before comparing, note three points:

- **Seibert does not cite this paper.** Its challenge is to earlier assignments of stripes to nitrogen, and it cites other papers from the same group: Lu et al., *Langmuir* (2012) and *Appl. Surf. Sci.* (2014).
- **Different gas.** Lu et al. attribute the ordered patterns they image mainly to oxygen accumulating at the interface.
- **Different conditions.** Their water was pressurised with oxygen or nitrogen at 2.4–3.7 atm and injected with a disposable syringe (Methods).

13. Identify the composition claim in Lu et al. (2014) and the evidence in Seibert (2020) that bears on it. State whether the later evidence concerns the identical specimen, the identical recorded image, or a comparable phenomenon under different conditions. Preserve both claims and their publication dates. Leave unknown acquisition dates unknown.
14. Explain why revising a physical interpretation is different from revising a claim that two images show the same region.
